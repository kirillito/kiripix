## Signed off (2026-11-11)

- **The UI:** "much better performance and responsiveness". Lifting a Layer is still the longest task, but its progress bar
  shows it's working.
- **Time per room:** a room of average complexity from scratch takes about 40 minutes on a GPU, and about 15 with Claude doing
  the first pass (M2: 75 minutes for r03 by hand). On a machine without a good GPU it's hard to measure: one Layer lift ran 6
  minutes before the Owner gave up. Not a problem, since asset prep will run on a machine with a GPU.
- **Time per character:** 120 minutes in all for mc02. That covers several attempts to generate the 3D Model in Blender, first
  with Opus and then with Fable (Fable 5.2 did much better than expected), reviewing and choosing, the Mixamo upload, finding
  the animations, auto-rigging and downloading, and running it through Kiripix. The journal span is below. A base collection
  of Models should make the next characters much faster.
- **What's next for Models:** the Owner will generate one male and one female base Model with Fable for the two main
  characters. mc02 is good but will be revised: larger eyes, no backpack, and the base body in underwear only (which suits M5's
  character layers, [0152](../m5/0152-m5-character-layers.md)). Generating Models in Blender this way may become part of making
  a character, at least for one with many animations.
- **In Godot:** "Characters in the game look very impressive." Four things to adjust, each now a ticket:
  1. Some clips are longer than needed, and a decided clip can't be edited (Mixamo's idle is 100 frames here; the game wants
     10-20, played on a timer once the player stops): [0156](../m4/0156-m4-edit-a-clip.md) and
     [0157](../m4/0157-m4-ui-edit-a-clip.md).
  2. There's no transition between clips, so an idle snaps into the attack:
     [0158](../m4/0158-m4-transition-clips-trial.md).
  3. The eyes vanish in some frames. The Owner will make them bigger in the Model (anime style, as in Sea of Stars or Chrono
     Trigger); a Check can say when a part vanishes: [0159](../m4/0159-m4-part-vanishes-check.md).
  4. The lighting looks great, but the character reads as pixelated 3D beside the painted rooms. A flatter, more 2D look
     is a trial: [0160](../m4/0160-m4-flatter-character-look-trial.md).

For the Godot session:
- **What export writes:** under `export.godot.root`, `character/<name>/` holds `<name>.tscn`, `frames.tres` (a
  `SpriteFrames`), `texture.tres` (a `CanvasTexture`), `color.png` and `normal.png`. The Manifest is
  `manifest/character.<name>.json`, at schema 2, with `ids.clips`.
- **The scene:** a `Node2D` root at the feet, with an `AnimatedSprite2D` child named `Sprite`. Animations are named
  `<clip>_<dir>`, so a test script plays them with `$Sprite.play("walk_sw")`. The directions are
  `s sw w nw n ne e se`.
- **The normal map:** 0113 checked headlessly, with Godot 4.7.2 at `C:\Homespace\[software]\Godot\`, that it survives
  `AtlasTexture` over `CanvasTexture` under a `PointLight2D`. The check passed in both Compatibility and Forward+. The lit
  look in each direction is still yours to judge.
- **The outline:** 1 px, outside the silhouette, in `finish`'s `outline_colour`. That is an index into the Project palette,
  and it defaults to 0. `placement=edge` is the alternative. A pose that reaches the cell's edge has no line on that side.
- **Pivot drift:** `sheet.pivot_drift` has a tolerance of 0. A stationary clip's pivot is now the feet in every frame
  ([0154](0154-m3-stationary-clip-pivot-follows-the-hips.md)), not the swaying hips, so the idle can't shift by a pixel. The
  cell grows to hold every pose ([0155](0155-m3-attack-overflows-the-cell.md)), so a lunge isn't cut and keeps its outline.
- **In the UI:** the frame strip, compass, player and onion skin are on `render`, `palette`, `finish` and `sheet`. The keys
  are: number pad for direction; `,` and `.` to step a frame; Up and Down to change clip; Space or `P` to play; `O` for onion
  skin; `N` for normals; `Z` for zoom. The normal view shows the normal frames only. It has no light that follows the mouse.
